Privacy Policy for Study and Notify

Last updated: 5 October 2026 (effective the same day)
App: Study and Notify (Android, package name com.floofandprickles.studynotify)
Developer: Floof and Prickles
Contact: floofandprickles@gmail.com

The short version

  • Your study material, reminders, tasks, ratings and settings stay on your phone. The developer cannot see them, and they are never sent to any server.
  • There is no account and no sign-in.
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. To show ads, Google's ad software in the app collects some information about your device, such as your advertising ID, your IP address (which can show your approximate location) and how you interact with ads. Google uses it for ads, measurement and fraud prevention, as explained in Google's policy.
  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (and anywhere else the law requires it), the app asks for your consent before ads load. You can change your choice at any time in Settings > Ad privacy choices.
  • You can remove ads with a subscription or a one-time purchase. Every feature stays free either way. Payments are made through Google Play, and the purchase is checked by RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous ID and your purchase details, but never your notes.
  • A file only leaves the app when you choose to export or back up, and only goes where you send it.
  • The developer does not sell your information.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy explains how the Study and Notify app handles information. "We" and "the developer" mean Floof and Prickles, who make the app. "You" means the person using the app.

2. What the app stores on your phone

The app keeps the following on your device so it can work:

  • Your study material: the text you paste in, split into items, plus the groups you organise them in (group names, which groups are on or off).
  • Your review history: the ratings you give each item ("Got it", "Partly", "Forgot" or your own labels) and the weights the app uses to decide how often an item comes back.
  • Your reminders: their titles, notes, times, repeat days, end dates and whether they are pinned.
  • Your daily tasks: task names, the days they are due, which days you completed them, and your streaks.
  • Your settings: review interval, quiet hours, rating-button labels, theme and dark mode, and similar choices.
  • Small technical records: for example, whether you have removed ads and when an ad was last shown (so ads are not shown too often).

All of this is saved in the app's private storage on your phone. Other apps cannot read it, and it is never sent to the developer, to Google's ad service, to RevenueCat or to any other server.

3. Ads in the free version (Google AdMob)

What is shown

The free version shows a full-screen ad from time to time (at most once every few minutes) right after you save something new, such as study material or a reminder. Ads are never shown inside notifications. If you have removed ads, the app does not show or request ads.

What Google collects

The ads are provided by Google AdMob, a service of Google. Google's ad software inside the app (the Google Mobile Ads SDK) collects and shares with Google, automatically:

  • Device identifiers: your Android advertising ID and app set ID (and, if applicable, identifiers linked to Google accounts signed in on the device);
  • your IP address, which can be used to estimate your general (approximate) location, such as your city or region. The app itself does not use your phone's location (GPS);
  • how you interact with ads and the app, such as app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information, such as how quickly the app and ads load and how much battery they use.

Google uses this information to show ads, to measure how ads perform, and to prevent fraud and abuse. Depending on your choices and your country, ads may be personalised (based on your interests) or non-personalised (based only on general information such as the app and your approximate location). Google sends this information over an encrypted connection.

How Google uses this information is explained in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and in Google's Privacy Policy. The developer does not receive this information about you personally; AdMob only gives the developer overall reports (for example, how many ads were shown in total).

Your consent and choices

  • EEA, UK and Switzerland: before any ads load, the app shows a consent message from Google (Google's User Messaging Platform, a Google-certified consent tool). You can accept, refuse, or choose which purposes and ad companies to allow. If you refuse, you may still see ads, but they will be non-personalised or "limited" ads.
  • US states with privacy laws (for example California): you can opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of your personal information for targeted advertising using the message the app shows in Settings > Ad privacy choices.
  • Change your mind at any time: open the app's Settings > Ad privacy choices to see the consent message again and change your choices. (The option appears where your region requires it.)
  • Your advertising ID: you can reset or delete it in your phone's settings, usually Settings > Google > Ads or Settings > Privacy > Ads (the exact path depends on your phone). After you delete it, apps receive a string of zeros instead of an ID.
  • Ad personalisation for your Google account: you can manage it at My Ad Center.
  • No ads at all: remove ads with a purchase (section 4). Every feature is available without paying.

4. Removing ads: purchases through Google Play and RevenueCat

You can remove ads with a monthly subscription, a yearly subscription or a one-time lifetime purchase. Paying only removes ads; every feature is free for everyone.

  • Google Play handles the payment. You pay with your Google account through Google Play's own purchase screen. Google Play's Privacy Policy and terms apply. The developer never sees your card or bank details. Google gives the developer limited order details (for example the order number, the product, the price, the date and the country of purchase), which the developer uses only for sales records, refunds and support.
  • RevenueCat checks the purchase. The app uses RevenueCat, a service that checks with Google Play that your purchase is real and tells the app that ads should be removed. RevenueCat receives:
    • an anonymous app user ID: a random code the app creates on your phone. It is not your name, email or Google account, and it changes if you reinstall the app;
    • purchase information: which product you bought, the price and currency, the purchase and renewal dates, and Google Play's purchase token and order number;
    • basic technical information: device type, Android version, app version, and your language and country settings; your IP address is also seen as part of the internet connection.
    RevenueCat processes this information on the developer's behalf, only to verify and manage purchases and to give the developer sales statistics. It does not receive your notes, reminders or tasks, and it is not used for ads. See RevenueCat's Privacy Policy. The information is sent over an encrypted connection.
  • No account needed. Your purchase is linked to the Google account you used in Google Play. If you reinstall the app or change phones, use Restore purchases in the app to get it back.
  • Cancelling a subscription: open the Google Play app, tap your profile picture, then Payments & subscriptions > Subscriptions, choose Study and Notify and tap Cancel subscription. Uninstalling the app does not cancel a subscription.

5. What the app does not do

Study and Notify does not:

  • ask you to create an account or sign in;
  • ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or your phone's location (GPS);
  • send your study material, reminders, tasks, ratings or settings anywhere;
  • use analytics or crash-reporting services of its own;
  • sell your information.

6. Exporting, backing up and importing (only when you choose)

  • Export and backup. When you tap Export (for a group, your tasks, or a full backup), the app creates a file and opens Android's share menu. You then choose where it goes, for example a cloud drive, an email or a messaging app. After that, the file is handled by the service you picked and by that service's own privacy policy. The developer never receives a copy.
  • Import and restore. The app only reads a file when you pick it in Android's file picker. It reads that one file and nothing else on your phone.

Backup files are plain text (JSON). They are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere you trust.

7. Android's own device backup

Like most Android apps, Study and Notify allows Android's built-in backup. If you have turned on backup in your phone's settings, Android may include this app's data in your phone's backup to your own Google account. That backup is run by Google under your Google account settings, not by the developer, and the developer cannot access it. You can turn it off in your phone's settings (usually Settings > Google > Backup, depending on your phone).

8. Permissions the app asks for, and why

  • NotificationsPOST_NOTIFICATIONS
    To show your review items, reminders and the daily "tasks left" message. Android asks you before allowing this, and you can turn it off at any time.
  • Alarms & remindersSCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM
    So reminders can arrive at the exact clock time you set. You can switch this off in your phone's settings; reminders may then arrive a few minutes late.
  • Run at startupRECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED
    To set your notifications up again after your phone restarts.
  • Keep the phone awake briefly, and vibrateWAKE_LOCK VIBRATE
    To deliver notifications reliably and let them vibrate.
  • Foreground serviceFOREGROUND_SERVICE
    Added by Android's standard background-work library (WorkManager). The app uses that library for a short background job, roughly every 30 minutes, that books your upcoming notifications on the phone. It does not send anything anywhere.
  • Network accessINTERNET ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE
    To load ads in the free version and to check purchases with Google Play and RevenueCat. Your notes, reminders and tasks are never sent over the internet.
  • Advertising IDcom.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID
    Lets Google's ad software read your advertising ID to show and measure ads (see section 3). You can reset or delete the ID in your phone's settings.
  • Android ad servicesACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS
    Added by Google's ad software. They let it use Android's built-in privacy-preserving advertising features, where your phone has them and you have them turned on. You control these in your phone's privacy or ads settings.
  • Google Play billingcom.android.vending.BILLING
    So you can buy ad removal through Google Play.

The app does not ask for access to your location (GPS), contacts, camera, microphone, photos or your phone's general storage.

9. How long information is kept, and how to delete it

  • On your phone: your information stays until you delete it. Delete single items, groups, reminders or tasks inside the app, or delete everything by uninstalling the app or going to Settings > Apps > Study and Notify > Storage > Clear storage.
  • Purchase records at RevenueCat: kept while they are needed to manage your purchase and for the developer's sales and tax records. To ask for them to be deleted, email floofandprickles@gmail.com with the order number from your Google Play receipt email (it starts with "GPA."). The developer will delete your record at RevenueCat. If you still have an active subscription, cancel it in Google Play first, and note that after deletion you may need to use Restore purchases again.
  • Order records at Google Play: Google keeps these under Google's own policies; the developer may need to keep basic sales records for tax reasons.
  • Ad data at Google: controlled by Google. Use the choices in section 3 (reset or delete your advertising ID, change your consent, My Ad Center).
  • Any backup files you exported yourself are yours to keep or delete.

10. If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland

  • Why your information is used (legal bases): personalised ads and storing or reading information on your device for ads rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time in Settings > Ad privacy choices. Non-personalised or limited ads and fraud prevention rely on legitimate interests (paying for a free app and keeping ads safe). Checking and managing your purchase relies on performing the contract with you.
  • Your rights: you can ask to access, correct or delete your personal information, object to its use, or ask for a copy. Email floofandprickles@gmail.com. You also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.
  • International transfers: Google and RevenueCat may process information outside your country, including in the United States, under their own safeguards described in their privacy policies.

11. Security

Your study material is kept in the app's private storage, which Android protects from other apps, and is never sent over the internet. Information sent for ads and purchases travels over encrypted connections. Exported backup files are not encrypted, so store and share them with care.

12. Children

Study and Notify is not designed for or directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from children under 13. The app is meant for people aged 13 and over. Ads in the app are limited to content suitable for a general audience. If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child under 13 has used the app or made a purchase, contact floofandprickles@gmail.com and we will help, including deleting any purchase record at RevenueCat.
